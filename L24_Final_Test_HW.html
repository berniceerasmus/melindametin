A Letter to My Future Self

Dear future me,

By the time you read this, I hope you have improved your English a great deal. When I started this course six months ago, I could barely form complex sentences in English. Now I can talk about my opinions, discuss the future, compare things, and write structured paragraphs. It has not been easy. There were lessons when I felt frustrated and days when I wanted to give up. However, I kept going — and I am glad I did. If I had stopped when it was difficult, I would never have reached B1. My biggest achievement has been speaking more confidently. I used to be terrified of making mistakes, but now I understand that mistakes are part of learning. To my future self: keep going, keep trying, and never stop learning.

Languages Open Doors

Learning a second language is one of the most valuable things a young person can do. Not only does it open doors to new cultures, friendships, and opportunities — it also changes the way your brain works. Research shows that bilingual people are better at problem-solving and concentrating. They also develop stronger memories. English, in particular, is useful because it is spoken as a first or second language in over 100 countries. If you can communicate confidently in English, you will have access to a far wider world of education, travel, and work. Many of the best universities in the world teach in English. Many of the most exciting job opportunities require it. In my opinion, every young person who learns English is investing in their own future — and that is never a waste of time.
