Six Months of English

Learning a language takes time, patience, and dedication. At the beginning of this course, you were at level A2. You could understand simple sentences, talk about familiar topics, and describe things in basic English. Now, six months later, you are approaching B1. You have learned to use the present perfect to talk about experiences, the first conditional to discuss real possibilities, and a range of opinion phrases to express your ideas clearly. You have also practised reading, writing, and speaking at a more advanced level. If you continue practising every day, your English will keep improving. The most important thing now is not to stop — even five minutes of English every day will make a difference.

What Makes a Champion?

People often think that champions are born, not made. However, research into sport and performance tells a very different story. Studies show that the most successful athletes are rarely the most naturally talented — they are the most consistent. They train when they do not feel like it. They lose and come back stronger. They ask for feedback and act on it. Ana Silva, the volleyball player you read about earlier in this course, is a perfect example. After a devastating injury, she returned to the court after eighteen months and went on to play for the national team. If she had given up, she would never have achieved her dream. The same principle applies to language learning. If you have worked hard on this course, you have already shown the qualities of a champion.
