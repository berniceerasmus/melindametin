Lesson 17: Present Perfect

A2+ English Lesson · ~45 minutes

Warm-up

🗣️ Warm-up

Talk about these questions with your teacher:

  • Have you ever travelled to another country? Where did you go?
  • Have you ever eaten something very unusual? What was it?
  • Have you ever met a famous person? Who was it?

Presentation

Grammar Focus: Present Perfect

Form: Subject + have / has + past participle

Negative: Subject + haven't / hasn't + past participle

Question: Have / Has + subject + past participle?


Use: We use the present perfect to talk about:

  • Experiences in your life (with ever and never)
  • Actions that happened recently and are still relevant now
  • Actions that started in the past and continue now (with for and since)

I have visited Paris. (life experience)

She has never eaten sushi. (never = negative experience)

Have you ever seen that film? (question about experience)

He has lived here for ten years. (continuing situation)

They have just arrived. (very recent action)

Key Words

everin questions — at any time in your life
nevernot at any time
justa very short time ago
alreadysooner than expected
yetin negatives and questions — up to now
fora period of time (for two years)
sincea point in time (since 2019)

Match the sentences to the correct use of the present perfect.

1. I have just made coffee. a. Life experience
2. Have you ever been to Australia? b. Very recent action
3. She has never seen snow. c. Continuing situation
4. We have lived here since 2015. d. Negative experience
5. He has broken his arm twice. a. Life experience
6. We have just landed at the airport. b. Very recent action
7. I have worked here since 2019. c. Continuing situation
8. She has never ridden a horse. d. Negative experience
9. They have already left the party. b. Very recent action
10. He has played the guitar for six years. c. Continuing situation

Answers: 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c, 5-a, 6-b, 7-c, 8-d, 9-b, 10-c. Say the matches aloud with your teacher.

Write 5 sentences about your life experiences using the present perfect. Use ever, never, just, already, or yet in at least 3 of them.
